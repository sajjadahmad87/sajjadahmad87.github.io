Steam Trap Survey Template and Field Guide

Capture one consistent, reviewable record per trap—from identity and representative operating state through inspection evidence, condition classification, action and post-repair verification.

Beginner → Intermediate → Advanced workflow

  1. Beginner—build the register: assign a non-confidential survey ID and trap tag, then record the application, trap family and safely visible identification.
  2. Intermediate—capture comparable evidence: state the operating load, inspection time, measurement points, units, acoustic method and observations.
  3. Advanced—control decisions: classify the condition and confidence, prioritize risk, link the authorized action and verify the result under a representative load.

Prerequisite: review the Steam Trap Inspection and Troubleshooting Guide and the approved site method before collecting field data. The worksheet organizes evidence; it does not determine whether access or testing is safe.

Use one row per trap and inspection event

Do not merge several traps into one row. A separate record preserves each trap's application, operating state, findings and corrective-action history. If a trap is retested on a different date or under a materially different load, create a linked inspection record or preserve the earlier values through the site's controlled record system.

Use roles rather than unnecessary personal information. If a working copy may be shared outside the approved system, remove confidential asset references and commercial information first.

Field groups and intended use

Identity and application

  • Survey_ID / Trap_Tag: stable, non-confidential references that allow findings, work orders and retests to be traced.
  • Location_or_Area / Served_Equipment_or_Application: enough context to find the trap and understand its function without exposing restricted site information.
  • Trap_Family / Manufacturer_and_Model / Connection_Size: record only what is safely visible or available from approved records; never disturb insulation or guarding merely to complete a field.
  • Flow_Direction_Verified: state yes, no or not verified rather than assuming installation direction.

Operating context and installation evidence

  • Supply_Pressure_Context / Return_Pressure_Context: use approved measured or design information and state units in the entry. Do not invent pressure where it is unavailable.
  • Operating_State_or_Load: note start-up, steady representative load, light load, isolated, shut down or another controlled description.
  • Bypass_Position / Isolation_Valve_Status: record observed or approved status without operating a valve solely for the survey.
  • Installation_Observations: note permitted visual evidence such as orientation, support, drainage fall, external leakage or return arrangement.

Temperature, acoustic and symptom evidence

  • Measurement points: describe repeatable upstream and downstream locations rather than recording unexplained values.
  • Temperature_Unit: explicitly state °C or °F and keep units consistent. Surface temperature is supporting evidence, not proof of internal condition.
  • Acoustic_Method_and_Setting: identify the approved instrument method, contact point and relevant setting so a retest can be compared.
  • Visual_or_Operating_Symptoms: record observations such as slow heating, hammer, external leakage or unstable control without turning the symptom into an assumed root cause.

Classification, action and verification

Use controlled terms consistently. Recommended general classifications are normal under observed condition, suspected leaking, suspected blocked/restricted, system condition requires investigation and not testable/inconclusive. Site-approved terminology takes priority.

  • Confidence_Level: state why the evidence supports high, medium or low confidence, or use the site's approved scale.
  • Immediate_Risk_or_Priority: prioritize steam leakage, severe hammer, damaged pressure parts and unsafe access through the approved escalation process.
  • Recommended_Action: define the next controlled step—retest, engineering review, authorized repair or wider system investigation.
  • Work_Order_Reference / Retest_Date: connect diagnosis to execution and prevent an unverified replacement from appearing complete.
  • Post_Repair_Result: state whether the original symptom cleared under a representative condition and record remaining abnormalities.

Survey quality check

  • Confirm the trap identity and operating state before interpreting readings.
  • Record measurement location, unit, instrument method and time for repeatability.
  • Use more than one evidence channel where the approved method permits.
  • Mark conflicting or non-representative evidence as inconclusive and plan a controlled retest.
  • Separate diagnostic evidence from energy-loss and financial estimates.
  • Verify corrective work under a representative load and preserve the pre-work evidence.

Safety, privacy and engineering limitations

This worksheet is an educational record structure, not an approved inspection procedure, pressure-system certificate, work order, energy audit or authorization to break containment. It does not specify universal pressure, temperature, survey-frequency or acceptance limits.

Site and OEM requirements take priority: steam and condensate can cause fatal pressure-release and burn injuries. Follow approved site procedures, OEM manuals, PTW/LOTO, isolation and depressurization rules, risk assessments, suitable PPE and competent-person controls. Never loosen fittings, open strainers or dismantle a pressurized or hot trap to complete this worksheet.
