VFD Fundamentals for Maintenance Technicians

A variable frequency drive controls AC motor speed and torque by converting fixed-frequency incoming power into a controlled output. Understanding the power section, control signals and motor load makes VFD troubleshooting much faster and safer.

How a VFD works

Most industrial drives use three main stages: a rectifier converts incoming AC to DC, a DC bus smooths and stores energy, and an inverter switches that DC to create a variable-frequency, variable-voltage output to the motor. The control system adjusts this output according to speed reference, process demand and configured limits.

Inputs and commands

A VFD may receive commands from local keypad controls, digital inputs, analogue signals, PLC communication or a building/plant control network. When a motor does not run, verify both the power circuit and the command path. A healthy drive may be waiting for a missing run permissive or speed reference.

Common VFD faults

  • Overcurrent: possible jam, acceleration too fast, motor fault, short circuit or incorrect parameters.
  • Overvoltage: often associated with rapid deceleration or regenerative load.
  • Undervoltage: incoming supply dip, loose connection or DC-bus issue.
  • Overtemperature: blocked cooling path, failed fan, high ambient temperature or overload.
  • Ground fault: cable insulation, motor winding or moisture problem.
Do not reset repeatedly. Record the exact alarm code, operating condition, speed, current and recent machine event before clearing a trip.

Practical troubleshooting sequence

  1. Confirm safe isolation requirements before opening panels.
  2. Read the fault history and active status.
  3. Verify incoming voltage and phase condition.
  4. Check run command, permissives and speed reference.
  5. Inspect motor and driven equipment for mechanical restriction.
  6. Compare motor nameplate data with drive parameters.
  7. Check cooling fans, filters and ambient temperature.
  8. Test motor/cable insulation using a procedure suitable for disconnected drive output.

VFD preventive maintenance

Keep the enclosure and cooling path clean, inspect fans, verify terminal condition, review event logs and compare current trends. In dusty industrial environments, blocked filters can create repeated thermal trips. For critical drives, maintain configuration backups and document replacement settings.
