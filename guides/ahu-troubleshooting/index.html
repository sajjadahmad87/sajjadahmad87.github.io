AHU Troubleshooting: Poor Cooling and Airflow

An AHU complaint may come from load, airflow, heat transfer, moisture removal, distribution or control. This guide builds a repeatable evidence chain before settings or components are changed.

Beginner → Intermediate → Advanced learning path

Prerequisites: basic AHU component awareness, approved access and isolation practices, and familiarity with the unit's design duty or normal trend. A learner should finish able to:

  • Beginner: define the actual comfort or process complaint and separate air, thermal and control symptoms.
  • Intermediate: collect comparable temperature, humidity, pressure, airflow, fan, damper and valve evidence.
  • Advanced: test competing fault paths, control one change at a time and verify performance after repair.
Safety boundary: approved site procedures, OEM manuals, PTW/LOTO, electrical and mechanical isolation, stored-energy controls, working-at-height rules, risk assessments and competent-person authorization take priority. Do not open access doors against fan pressure, reach through guards, approach belts or rotating parts, disturb live panels, move fire/smoke dampers or use chemicals solely to complete a check.

1. Define the complaint and system boundary

Replace “poor cooling” with an observable statement: space temperature rises during a known load, supply airflow is weak at certain terminals, leaving-air temperature is unstable, humidity remains high, a filter alarm repeats, condensate overflows or the fan cannot hold static pressure. State when the symptom begins and whether all served zones are affected.

A whole-system symptom points toward AHU capacity, outdoor-air load, fan operation or a common control. One weak branch may indicate a damper, terminal, balancing or duct issue. One warm space with normal supply conditions can result from local load, distribution, return-air path or sensing rather than the AHU itself.

2. Capture a comparable operating baseline

Record the same variables at the same time. At minimum include date and time, operating mode, occupancy or process state, outdoor condition, return and supply air temperature, relevant humidity, fan command and feedback, filter pressure drop, damper positions, valve command and space condition. Note doors, extract systems or production heat sources that changed the load.

  • Use the same approved measurement points, units and calibrated instruments for comparisons.
  • Allow the system to reach a reasonably stable state after startup or a commanded change.
  • Compare with design, commissioning or healthy historical evidence—not an unrelated unit or a reading taken at a different load.
  • Preserve BMS trends and alarm timing before overrides or resets erase the sequence.

Do not invent a universal supply-air temperature, filter pressure, fan speed or humidity limit. The system design, approved ventilation basis, OEM data, filter selection and site acceptance criteria determine what is acceptable.

3. Trace the airflow path from intake to occupied zone

Outdoor and return-air paths

Check safely visible intake screens, louvers and mixing sections for obstruction or contamination. Compare outdoor, return and relief damper commands with physical movement where authorized. A displayed percentage is not proof that a linkage, blade or actuator reached the commanded position.

Filters

Record differential pressure using the installed instrument or an approved test method and compare it with the filter manufacturer's and site's change criteria. Inspect for collapsed media, wet filters, gaps, bypass around frames and incorrect installation. A visually dirty filter may still need measured context, while a clean-looking but wet or incorrectly fitted filter can create airflow and indoor-air-quality problems.

Fan and drive

Verify the proven run state, rotation, speed command, actual VFD frequency or feedback, belt condition and tension method, sheave or coupling condition, motor current context, vibration, bearing condition and abnormal noise. Compare fan inlet and discharge pressure evidence where test points and the approved procedure permit.

Do not compensate blindly: increasing VFD frequency can raise fan load, noise, leakage and pressure while hiding a blocked filter, closed damper, slipping belt or failed sensor. Establish the restriction and confirm motor, fan and system limits before changing speed.

Duct and terminal distribution

Check known isolation, balancing and terminal positions, flexible connections, access panels and permitted fire/smoke-damper status. Never force or override a life-safety damper. If total AHU airflow appears normal but a zone is weak, move the investigation downstream and verify branch or terminal airflow using the approved testing and balancing method.

4. Build thermal and moisture evidence

Record entering and leaving air dry-bulb temperature plus relevant humidity or dew-point evidence using appropriate instruments. Temperature reduction alone does not show whether moisture removal, airflow and total capacity are satisfactory. Very low airflow can produce a cold leaving-air reading while the space still receives too little total cooling.

Inspect the accessible coil face for dust, lint, bent fins, uneven wetting, frost or air bypass. Note whether fouling is uniform. A clean section beside a blocked section can create uneven leaving-air temperature and misleading spot readings.

For water-fed coils, record entering and leaving water temperatures, valve command and verified movement, pump/system availability, relevant differential pressure, strainer or balancing evidence and air-venting concerns where the approved design provides test points. A full valve command does not prove flow. A large or small temperature difference is not diagnostic without air quantity, load, measurement accuracy and design context.

5. Inspect condensate and moisture control

Where cooling produces condensate, inspect the pan, drain outlet, trap arrangement and permitted downstream path. Look for standing water, corrosion, overflow marks, damaged insulation, air leakage at the drain connection and evidence that pressure conditions are preventing drainage. Keep drain pans and wet surfaces clean through approved methods; use only authorized chemicals and disposal controls.

Standing water or recurring overflow needs a cause, not only removal. Check pan slope and condition, trap seal and dimensions against the unit pressure and approved design, drain blockage, air balance, coil carryover and shutdown/startup behavior. Post-cleaning verification should include operation at a representative condition.

6. Verify sensors, actuators and control sequence

Compare BMS values with an independent calibrated instrument at a representative location. Confirm sensor type, scaling, units, location, wiring condition and trend behavior. A correctly displayed value can still be unrepresentative if the sensor is affected by stratification, heat, moisture, air leakage or poor placement.

  • Command versus feedback: distinguish what the controller requests from what the valve, damper or fan actually does.
  • Interlocks: review fan proof, filter alarms, freezestat or safety states, occupancy schedules, fire/life-safety interfaces and plant availability without defeating them.
  • Control stability: trend setpoint, process value, output and feedback together. Hunting may come from tuning, sensor noise, actuator problems, valve authority, system lag or unstable load.
  • Overrides: identify manual or software overrides, who authorized them, their expiry and whether normal automatic control was restored.

Change one controlled variable at a time where practical, observe a sufficient response period and preserve the before/after trend. Replacing a sensor or actuator is not proof of correction until the complete sequence performs correctly.

7. Use evidence to separate common fault paths

  • Low airflow across the whole unit: investigate filter pressure, fan/drive feedback, common dampers, fan rotation, system pressure and major restrictions.
  • Normal airflow but weak thermal performance: investigate load, entering condition, coil condition, medium flow, valve movement and control demand.
  • Good AHU discharge but poor space condition: investigate distribution, terminal control, return path, local load, infiltration and space-sensor evidence.
  • Temperature acceptable but humidity high: review outdoor-air moisture load, airflow, coil leaving condition, control sequence, reheat or process sources as applicable to the design.
  • Intermittent complaint: correlate alarms, schedules, occupancy, extract operation, door activity, plant pressure, command/feedback and weather in a common timeline.

Keep at least two plausible hypotheses until the evidence distinguishes them. A dirty component, active alarm or fully open command may be relevant without being the root cause.

8. Verify the repair under representative duty

After authorized work, restore guards, access doors, filters, sensors, drains, control mode and overrides. Confirm fan rotation and stable operation, check for leakage and abnormal noise, then repeat the original measurements at comparable load and outdoor conditions where practical.

Acceptance should demonstrate that the original complaint cleared, the control sequence is stable, airflow and pressure evidence meet approved criteria, condensate drains correctly, no new alarm or zone imbalance appeared and energy use did not rise because a restriction was hidden with more fan speed. Record open limitations and schedule a trend review when immediate conditions are not representative.

Minimum AHU troubleshooting record

  • Unit, served area, complaint, event time, operating mode and load context.
  • Outdoor, return, mixed and supply condition at named measurement points.
  • Filter differential pressure, fan command/feedback, motor-current context and relevant static pressure.
  • Damper and valve commands, verified movement, interlocks, alarms and overrides.
  • Coil face, condensate pan, drain, belt/drive, duct and terminal observations.
  • Competing hypotheses, supporting and contradicting evidence, controlled action and approval reference.
  • Post-repair readings, representative-duty limitations and follow-up trend date.

Turn findings into preventive maintenance

Convert repeat faults into measurable tasks: inspect intake condition, record filter pressure, verify damper travel, inspect fan and drive condition, trend sensors against calibrated references, clean coils and pans using approved methods, confirm drain performance, review alarm history and test the sequence at defined intervals based on risk, environment and OEM/site requirements.

A checklist should state the point, method, unit, expected evidence, acceptance source and escalation action. “Check AHU” is not a defensible maintenance task.

Limitations and escalation

This guide does not replace design calculations, testing and balancing, controls commissioning, electrical testing, water treatment, indoor-air-quality investigation or competent engineering review. Escalate smoke or burning odor, exposed rotating parts, damaged guards, severe vibration, water near live equipment, contaminated porous material, suspected microbial growth, repeated safety trips, life-safety interface concerns or uncertainty about continued operation.

Authoritative further reading

  • US EPA: HVAC Systems and Indoor Air Quality — access, filtration, coils, drain pans and outdoor-air considerations.
  • ASHRAE Standards 62.1 and 62.2 overview — current ventilation and acceptable indoor-air-quality scope.
  • ASHRAE standards and guideline scopes — includes the purpose of field testing HVAC control components.
  • US DOE FEMP: Commissioning and Acceptance Guidance — functional verification and acceptance context.

These sources support further study. Use the current approved ventilation basis, drawings, sequence, commissioning records, OEM manuals and site criteria for the actual AHU.
